Evidence for investigation, not a threat verdict. A connection or registration alone does not establish necessity, safety or misuse. Counts represent collected source records, not packets, bytes or connections that remain active.
Understand DNS, UDP and registration data

DNS names are candidates. Several domains can share an IP address. DNS-cache and reverse-DNS data do not prove which website a process visited. Check the evidence in the connection details. UDP endpoints without a remote address describe a local socket; additional observation is needed to determine its peer. WHOIS via RDAP identifies the registrant of an address range or domain, not necessarily the service behind a connection. Registration country is not server geolocation. Missing data remains unknown.

Collection limitations

    Refine the selection. Active fields are combined with AND; comma-separated IPs, ports, PIDs and countries match any listed alternative.

    Process and executable

    Text fields match part of the saved value without case sensitivity. PIDs accept comma-separated numbers or ranges. Service descriptions come from Windows and may use the system language.

    Addresses, ports and direction

    IPs accept exact IPv4/IPv6 addresses or CIDR networks; separate alternatives with commas. Ports accept numbers and ranges. IP family uses the remote address, or the local address when the peer is missing.

    DNS and remote registration

    DNS and reverse-DNS names are candidates. Registration filters examine the remote IP and associated domain registrations. Country means registration country, not server location.

    Observation time and repetition

    Times are UTC, matching the table. A record matches when its first-to-last-observed interval overlaps the selected period. Observation counts are source sightings, not packet or byte counts.
